012023.09 — 2024.08

Mobile Robot Auto-Aiming System

RoboMaster 2024 Hero, Infantry, and Aerial robots

Vision Algorithm Team Lead · USTB Reborn Robotics Team

RoboMaster 2024: First Prize, Robot Competition Award (Aerial Robot); National Third Prize, University Championship

Background: Close a real-time loop of detection, state estimation, and aiming on competition robots, against enemy armor plates that spin and translate at the same time.

  • System: Built the team’s auto-aiming system on top of community open-source frameworks such as rm_vision and RMVL, and deployed it on the robots. It aims at both enemy armor plates and the power rune at the center of the field.
  • Detection: Armor plate detection with a YOLOv8-Pose four-keypoint network (MobileNetV3 backbone, OpenVINO inference).
  • Armor aiming: An extended Kalman filter estimates the state of the whole target vehicle; the system predicts linearly, selects which armor plate to hit, solves the trajectory with an air-drag model, and outputs gimbal target angles.
  • Power rune aiming: Detects the target blade and the central R mark, then fits the unknown parameters of the rune’s speed model online with the Levenberg–Marquardt method, within the parameter ranges given by the rules. The same air-drag trajectory model produces the gimbal target angles.
  • Season results: Over 80% hit accuracy within 6 m against targets spinning at 2 rad/s and translating at 1 m/s; hit the power rune in official matches.
  • Team management: Managed a seven-person algorithm team: set season goals, tracked progress, standardized technical documentation, and coordinated with the other groups of the team.

022022.09 — 2023.08

Swerve-Drive Engineer Robot with a Manipulator

RoboMaster 2023 Engineer robot

Mechanical Design, Vision-Assist Algorithms, Robot Operator · USTB Reborn Robotics Team

RoboMaster 2023: First Prize, Robot Combat Award (Engineer Robot); National Second Prize, University Championship

Background: Design and build the team’s Engineer robot, which picks up and places “ore” on the field. Ore lies in random orientations and the exchange slot changes pose across several difficulty levels, so the end effector of the arm has to adjust its pose accordingly.

  • Mechanical design: Designed a four-wheel swerve-drive chassis with independent suspension and the upper manipulation mechanism to meet the competition rules; assembled and tested the full robot.
  • Swerve chassis control: Inverse kinematics from body velocity commands to steering angle and wheel speed per module; forward-kinematics estimation for closed-loop body velocity control and for slip and lift-off detection; engineering refinements such as steering command optimization and wheel speed saturation limits to improve dynamic performance and stability.
  • Vision-assisted arm alignment: Feature point extraction, then PnP with bundle adjustment to solve the pose of field props.
  • Robot operator: Operated the robot in matches and was responsible for it on the field; named team MVP of the match three times.

032026.05 — 2026.11

Teleoperation System for a Dual-Arm Wheeled Humanoid

Background: Teleoperating dual-arm humanoids is hard to learn and much slower than a human doing the task directly. Two places to improve: how images from the robot’s head are transmitted to and presented in the VR headset, and force feedback, which makes contact-rich tasks easier to complete.

  • Approach: An immersive teleoperation system for a dual-arm humanoid on a wheeled base, plus human takeover between teleoperation and learned policies.
  • My work: Led the system architecture, the follower-side design, and motion control: VR headset and controller poses mapped to the two arm end effectors; component-based ROS 2 architecture with 1 kHz joint and Cartesian impedance control; integration of six-axis force sensing and force-feedback devices; safety mechanisms.
  • Learning: Trained and deployed a residual model that corrects teleoperation commands.
  • Results: Demo completed in 1.5 months (VR dual-arm teleoperation, force-feedback contact tasks, residual model deployment) with zero incidents on the real robot. Agents did most of the coding; I was responsible for architecture, review, and acceptance testing on hardware.

2024.10 — 2025.06

Small Pneumatic Worm-Like Climbing Robot with Suction Adhesion

Outstanding Undergraduate Thesis · Advisors: Prof. Jianhong Yang, Prof. Chao Wang · Second author of an IROS 2025 paper

Problem: Narrow and unstructured spaces need inspection for defects and faults, but existing mobile robots cannot combine agile motion and wall adhesion within centimeter-scale size limits. Soft pneumatic actuators are hard to constrain in every direction of deformation, and bellows actuators are still designed largely by experience, without a mapping between structural parameters and performance.

  • Robot design: Designed an earthworm-inspired robot with pneumatic actuation and adhesion: vacuum suction modules at the front and rear, and a drive module of bellows-type pneumatic actuators in parallel. Rigid parts are 3D printed, the actuators are cast in silicone in steps, and I built the matching pneumatic control system.
  • Kinematics: Whole-robot kinematics based on piecewise constant curvature theory; Monte Carlo sampling to analyze the workspace and design gaits.
  • Actuator parameters: Combined finite element simulation and measurement to study how six parameters (Shore hardness, number of units, wall thickness, mean diameter, unit width, unit depth) affect actuator deformation. The resulting mapping between structural parameters and performance of bellows actuators was published at IROS 2025.
  • Role in the paper: Built the experimental equipment, ran the measurements, analyzed and processed the data, wrote the experiment section, and shot and edited the video.
  • Results: Prototype with a maximum diameter of 84 mm, crawling speed up to 30 cm/min on flat surfaces, up to 45° of turning per cycle, and a maximum climbable slope of 75°.
Soft roboticsPiecewise constant curvatureFinite element simulationPneumatic control

Paper: W. Zou, Z. Li, Z. Xiao, K. Zheng, C. Lin, H. Chen, J. Jiang*, Y. Niu*, C. Wang*. Parameter Selections and Applications for Soft Bellows Actuators (SBAs) with Various Performance Metrics. IEEE/RSJ International Conference on Intelligent Robots and Systems (IROS), 2025.

2023.11 — 2024.07

Adaptive High Dynamic Range Imaging for High-Temperature Full-Field Deformation Measurement

University of Science and Technology Beijing · Advisor: Prof. Jianhong Yang · Second author

Problem: In laser speckle digital image correlation (DIC), curved surfaces and uneven temperature across hot specimens create large luminance gradients, so a single exposure cannot keep the speckle in both bright and dark regions.

  • Method: Adaptive high dynamic range imaging fusion, with a criterion for choosing exposure parameters that avoids motion blur; validated in a uniaxial tensile test of a nickel-based superalloy bar at 1300 °C.
  • My work: Took part in experiment design and algorithm discussions, collected and processed experimental data, and drafted the review and experiment sections. Also developed the group’s spin-off product, a DIC digital extensometer: mechanical design, front-end interface, and packaging and debugging of the back-end algorithm library.
Digital image correlationHDR imagingHigh-temperature testing

Paper: F. Liu, Z. Li, J. Yang*, et al. An adaptive high dynamic range imaging fusion method to overcome high luminance gradient in full-field deformation measurement using laser speckle digital image correlation. Optics and Lasers in Engineering.
